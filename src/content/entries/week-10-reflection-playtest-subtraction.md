## What we're changing

After the playtest we were pretty clear about the problem. The design was too much, and it was our idea of how a kid should play. Next week we're cutting. Some parts are interesting and we are not going to get them built in time, or built well enough to trust. If children can't actually play it, it comes out.

The drum is the example we keep coming back to. We thought it would be a fun input. In the test it got squeezed and hit until it broke. It was fine only when someone was gentle and played it the way we had pictured. That lines up with what Bal, Nolan, and Seko argue about children as hackers. Kids already learn by changing a situation an adult set up: making a mess, making noise, trying "what would happen if." Squeezing the drum was that. We had designed an input for a child who would leave it alone. The next one has to stay in one piece while they play with it their own way.

The topic needs the same kind of cut. "Sustainability" didn't mean much to them. It was a big word with nothing to point at. We're moving the activity onto things they already see, electricity at home and exhaust from cars. Then the input has a job. It can be about a light left on, or a car going by, which is a situation they recognize.

## What we still need to figure out

We want to read a bit on how children actually behave with objects, and which inputs they go for, before we build the sturdy version. Hummels and van Dijk's idea of a scaffold is the test we want to use. A scaffold is something in the room that shows you the next move, so a person doesn't have to stop and explain a rule. Right now our device would need us standing there saying be gentle, press this, wait. The new input should make the next touch obvious by how it feels and what it does back.

## The class around it

We also haven't really designed the class, only the object. Next week we need to decide how this game sits in a real classroom. What do the kids see first, what do they get to try, and what is the teacher doing while it runs. If the teacher has to spend the period explaining our rules, the scaffold isn't in the object yet. The teacher should be able to teach the home-electricity or car-exhaust idea with the device, and the device should be able to take the rough, curious use we already watched.
