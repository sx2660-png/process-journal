Today we completed our drill press training in the makerspace. The session covered two main operations: cutting through wooden boards and drilling holes at precise locations. We learned how to secure the workpiece on the base plate, adjust the drill depth, control feed speed, and manage the machine safely — including when to stop if the bit catches.

What I found interesting is that this was technically a "re-learning" experience for me. My undergraduate thesis was a wooden mechanical installation, so I've worked with similar tools before. But coming back to them in a new space made me realize how much of that knowledge is physical rather than verbal — I didn't remember the steps, I remembered the feel. The resistance when the bit enters the wood, the sound shift when it breaks through the other side. That's not something you can read your way into.

The makerspace itself has a clear workflow and safety culture, which is new to me. Compared to the workshop I used during my undergrad, the setup here is more formalized — safety labels, designated tool zones, a training requirement before you touch anything. It slows you down at first, but it also makes the space feel more trustworthy.

Looking forward to putting these skills to use in upcoming projects.
